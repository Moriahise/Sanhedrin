Machluf Elbaz Chacham Dayan

Question

Kiss a Torah Tablet?

There are tablets sold, eg by artscroll, preloaded with Torah and have blocked all access to anything else except things that aid learning like alarms etc.

Is it appropriate, or inappropriate to kiss this tablet when using it like a sefer? Is it even permitted?

Answer

The sources that speak about kissing describe a custom tied to the Sefer Torah when it is carried or shown to the congregation, and the reason given is education and honor for the Torah scroll, not a rule about any object containing Torah text. Therefore, these sources support respect while using the tablet, but not a claim that kissing it is an established Sefer-Torah practice. [1] [3]

But other authorities in the same material already limit or criticize how that is done, showing that even with a real Sefer Torah the practice is a custom of honor, not an absolute requirement. [3]

The sources also show that words of Torah themselves are not susceptible to impurity, and even people in states of ritual impurity may hold and read a Sefer Torah. That tells us the holiness in these sources is not a physical impurity-status that can simply be transferred from Torah content to every object that displays it. [4]

What is clearly supported is general kavod haTorah: one should sit before Torah with seriousness, honor it as much as possible, and honor Torah study. So if someone uses the tablet for learning, treating it respectfully is appropriate. [4] [3]

There is also a useful distinction in the sources between the sacred text and the physical object holding it. [4]

If one wants to show respect while learning from it, that is supported in a general way by kavod haTorah; but equating it with a Sefer Torah is not supported here. [5]

Still open

  • No source here directly addresses a digital tablet or whether Torah text displayed electronically gives the device the status of a Sefer Torah or printed sefer.
  • No source here gives a specific ruling for kissing an electronic Torah tablet as a custom.

Containing 1 S'if: The congregation does not have permission to leave the synagogue until after they conceal the Torah scroll. (However, if they are all not leaving, but it's just an individual that is, then that is not an issue) (Beit Yosef in the name of the R'i in Chidushei Megillah). And if they conceal the Torah in another house: if the synagogue has only one entrance, then they must delay until the Torah scroll has left and then they should go after it to the place in which they conceal it. If the synagogue has 2 entrances, they may leave using one entrance before the Torah scroll leaves using the other entrance, as long as they will go after the sefer Torah and escort it to the place where they conceal it. Gloss: And in places where they conceal it in the "heikhal", which is the cabinet [i.e. Ark] within the synagogue, it is a obligation for anyone in front of whom it [i.e. the Torah] passes to escort it up until [it is] in front of the Ark into which they are inserting it (His own opinion, and Mahari"l). And so too, the one who rolls the sefer Torah should go after the Torah until in front of the Ark and stand there until they return the Torah to its place. (Hagahot Maimoni - chapter 12 of Hilkhot Torah). And similarly, we practice that the one who lifts the sefer Torah [follows as well] since this is [considered] the main "roller" as we explained in Siman 147: S'if 4. And there are those who have written that the children come to kiss the Torah in order to educate them and train them to be zealous in [doing] mitzvot, and that is how we practice. (Ohr Zaruah)
