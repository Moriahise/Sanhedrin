Question

Memallel vs. M'daber vs. M'Siach

Related to this question and the comments on it, because originally by mistake I called the speaking kingdom Medaber instead of Memallel.

Are porpoises considered Memallel or Chai?

Why is Memallel chosen to be the label for the fourth kingdom? I take it part of it is that speech that is not heard or accepted as per below, is not called Dibbur, so I can see what Medaber is. Sicha is casual conversation. Mila refers to words.

https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Likutei_Etzot#DIBBUR_-_Speech

Speech (Dibbur) that doesn't get heard or accepted can't be called "speech" at all. Tikkun haDibbur, when it's heard and accepted, comes by saying words of praise of the True Tzaddikim. This is Tikkun haKlali, General Remedy, for your speech (29:1,2).
But what is Memallel versus now there's Dinbur and Mila so two things to differentiate from, and the answer coming to me, that Memallel is one who utters words. Medaber is someone whose speech (or written words?) is heard or accepted. M'Siach is someone who converses, that you can chat with. Is this correct, or no?

Also, Dibbur can refer to written speech, so one could be illiterate yet still speaks the words of the language and is therefore Memallel. I guess M'daber is therefore more inclusive, because it includes also up someone who writes but doesn't speak. Since speech came and comes before writing, I guess I've answered my own question. Unless I'm mistaken

Answer

Onkelos renders Adam as a "speaking spirit" / "living [speaking] soul," [1]

The Zohar and the later sources cited from it use "rucha memallala" for the soul that ascends and speaks before the Holy King; Avodat HaKodesh then develops this as an "inner speech" tied to thought, wisdom, and completion. So in these sources, "memallel" is not just any utterance; it is a technical label for the speaking capacity that belongs to the higher human soul or to inner spiritual articulation. [3] [4]

"medaber" is the ordinary designation for the human level in the creation hierarchy. One source explicitly says that the human being is called "medaber," because speech is what distinguishes him from animals. your instinct that "medaber" is the broader human category is supported [6] [14]

Yein Levanon and Midrash Shmuel say that "sicha" refers to trivial, needless, or flirtatious talk, while normal useful discussion is called "dibbur." So "sicha" is not a synonym for speech in general; it is a subcategory of speech, usually with a negative or at least casual connotation. [8] [9]

The idea that speech not heard or accepted is "not speech at all" appears in the cited Likutei Etzot excerpt, but the source there is a spiritual teaching about repairing speech, not a general dictionary rule. Avodat HaKodesh likewise speaks about speech, action, and thought being perfected when they are directed properly. [13] [15]

one source says writing is a "messenger" of speech, and another says the written word is more lasting than spoken words. So the texts support a close relation between speech and writing, but not that writing is literally the same as speech in ordinary usage. [6]

and to inner articulation before the Divine Throne as a higher spiritual expression of that feature. They do not give an explicit reason for the naming choice beyond that distinction between man and the other living creatures. [3] [14]

Still open

  • No supplied source identifies porpoises specifically, so their classification cannot be decided from these texts alone.
  • No supplied source gives a general lexical definition of "medaber" as "heard/accepted speech"; that distinction appears only in the quoted Hypothesis in the question and in the spiritual usage of Avodat HaKodesh / Likutei Etzot, not as an ordinary dictionary definition.
  • Paragraph 1 overstates the sources; they do not support a complete answer about porpoises or a broad lexical rule for "medallel/medaber."
  • Paragraph 7 is supported only for the speech/writing distinction; it should not imply writing is literally speech in ordinary usage.

[11] Does determining the proper action in scenario X count towards Talmud Torah?

miyodeya · Y &#160; &#160; e &#160; &#160; z

The Maharal in Nesiv HaTorah towards the end of chapter 4 writes that one does not need to make a Birkas haTorah on thoughts of Torah. The Maharal explains that this is because a person is defined by speech, as Onkelos translates ויהי האדם לנפש חיה, Man became a living being, as רוח ממללא, a speaking being. Man is defined by the intersect between the intellect and the body, which is exemplified in the bringing out of the thought into speech, which requires the combination of intellect and action (this is expanded in Gevuros Hashem ch. 28). The intellect, and therefore thought, is not really part of the person (this is expanded on in ch. 9), and therefore involvement in Torah in thought is not really the person himself being involved. It is only through speech that the person is considered to himself be involved in Torah. The Maharal does not explicitly say that this is not considered Talmud Torah, but the implication of not requiring the Birkas haTorah implies that this is not a fulfillment of the mitzvah of Talmud Torah.
