Question

Who does the prophecy in Malachi refer to?

Malachi 3:1:

הִנְנִ֤י שֹׁלֵ֙חַ֙ מַלְאָכִ֔י וּפִנָּה־דֶ֖רֶךְ לְפָנָ֑י וּפִתְאֹם֩ יָב֨וֹא אֶל־הֵיכָל֜וֹ הָאָד֣וֹן ׀ אֲשֶׁר־אַתֶּ֣ם מְבַקְשִׁ֗ים וּמַלְאַ֨ךְ הַבְּרִ֜ית אֲשֶׁר־אַתֶּ֤ם חֲפֵצִים֙ הִנֵּה־בָ֔א אָמַ֖ר יְהֹוָ֥ה צְבָאֽוֹת׃

Behold, I am sending My messenger to clear the way before Me, and the Sovereign you seek shall come to the temple suddenly. As for the angel of the covenant that you desire, he is already coming.

Who is "My messenger"? (Elijah?) Who is "the Sovereign you seek"? (The Messiah?) Who is "the angel of the covenant"?

[1] Who does the prophecy in Malachi refer to?

miyodeya · Dov

A few possibilities... Note the Ibn Ezra , he suggests the messenger could possibly be Moshiach ben Yosef. The Metzudos Dovid provides a definitive rundown. He suggests the messenger to be a "מלאך מן השמים" - an angel from heaven, and he will clear the way before G-d. He will cut off from the world the sinners and the rebels, before Hashem purifies the land with His Shechinah. As far as the Sovereign, he writes: זהו מלך המשיח אשר עין כל אדם מצפה ומייחל לו ומבקש ביאתו This is the Messianic King whom the eye of every man expects and longs for and longs for his coming And the malach habris is a reference to Eliyahu.

[3] Malbim on Malachi 3:1

Sefaria

he: הנני שלח משיב להם מה שאתם רוצים שיתגלה אלהי המשפט ויעניש תיכף את הרשעים ויתן שכר לצדיקים, זה אי אפשר שיהיה בזה"ז, כי עתה הוא עדיין עולם הבחירה והנסיון וצריך שיתנהג הכל בדרך הטבע כדי שיהיה הבחירה נתונה לעשות טוב ורע ויהיה מקום לנסיון ולשכר ועונש, שאם יתגלה השי"ת בכבודו אז לא יצוייר עוד שיעשה איש מעשה רע, כי אז אף על חטא קל יענש תיכף ויעבור מן העולם ויתיראו מעשות רע, וע"כ הדרך הזה שילך ה' בעצמו אל יצוריו לשפטם ולהנהיגם על זה האופן, לא נסלל עדיין ולא הוכן בזה הזמן, רק אחר שיתוקן העולם אז הנני שולח מלאכי ופנה דרך זה לפני, שתחלה ילך נביא בשם ה' אשר יפנה דרך זה מכל אבני מכשול, ר"ל שיתקן את העולם בתשובה, ואז כשיהיה העולם מתוקן והדרך מוכן, אז פתאום יבא אל היכלו האדון אשר אתם מבקשים, אז יבא ה' לשכון בהיכלו למטה ולהתהלך בתוככם, ואז מלאך הברית אשר אתם חפצים הנה כבר בא, כי המלאך יקדים לבא קודם בא האדון ה' צבאות, והוא המלאך שיכניס את העולם בברית התורה והאמונה ובברית בינם לבין אלהים, וחז"ל פי' שזה אליהו מלאך הברית שיקדים לבא להשיב רבים מעון והוא אשר קנא על הברית בשטים ובימי אחאב: en: Behold, I send. He answers them: What you desire—that the God of justice reveal Himself, immediately punish the wicked, and reward the righteous—cannot occur in the present age. For the world is now still a realm of free choice and trial, and everything must proceed according to the way of nature so that people remain free to do good or evil and there remains room for trial, reward, and punishment. If the Holy One, blessed be He, were to reveal Himself in His glory, it would no longer be conceivable for anyone to commit an evil deed, since even for a minor sin he would be punished immediately and removed from the world, and all would fear to do evil. Therefore, this path—by which Hashem Himself will go forth to His creatures to judge them and govern them in this manner—has not yet been cleared or prepared in the present age. Only after the world has been perfected will “Behold, I send My messenger, and he shall clear this path before Me.” First, a prophet will go forth in Hashem’s name and clear this path of every stumbling block—that is, he will bring the world to repentance. Then, once the world has been perfected and the path prepared, “Hashem whom you seek will suddenly come to His Temple.” Hashem will then come to dwell in His Temple below and walk among you. At that time, “the messenger of the covenant whom you desire—behold,” he has come, for the messenger will arrive before Hashem, Hashem of Hosts. He is the messenger who will bring the world into the covenant of Torah and faith and into the covenant between humanity and God. Our Sages explained that this is Elijah, the messenger of the covenant, who will come first to turn many away from sin and who displayed zeal for the covenant at Shittim and in the days of Ahab.

[5] Why Is There a Chair for Elijah at a Brit Milah (Circumcision)?

chabad · Yehuda Shurpin

At the circumcision ceremony (brit milah), it is customary to have a seat designated for Elijah the prophet. According to tradition, Elijah, who is referred to as the “angel of the brit,”1 visits every circumcision ceremony. Here’s why: During the period when the nation of Israel was split into two kingdoms, Elijah the prophet had a showdown at Mt. Carmel with 450 false prophets of the Baal idol, resulting in their deaths. The king of the northern kingdom, Ahab, together with his wicked wife Jezebel, sent a message to the prophet Elijah: “At this time tomorrow, I will make your life like the life of one of them.”2 Elijah fled, and 40 days later he arrived at a cave on Mount Sinai where he spent the night. There G‑d revealed himself to Elijah and said, “Elijah, why are you here?” Elijah replied: “I have been zealous for the L‑rd, the G‑d of Hosts, for the children of Israel have forsaken Your covenant . . . and they seek my life to take it.”3 Which covenant did the people forsake? The Midrash says Elijah was referring to circumcision, which is called a brit (“covenant”). Furthermore, according to one midrash, not only did Elijah complain about them abandoning the covenant, but in his zealotry he asked G‑d to punish the sinners of Israel.4 The Midrash says that G‑d rebuked him, saying: “They abandoned My covenant, not yours; they dismantled My altars, not yours; they killed My prophets, not yours. Why are you more zealous than I!?5 “I vow,” continued G‑d, “that whenever My children make this sign in their flesh [i.e., whenever there is a circumcision], you will be present, and the mouth which testified that the Jewish people have abandoned My covenant will testify that they are keeping it."6 By attending every circumcision, Elijah was given the opportunity to rectify his error by testifying in their favor and to their credit that they do keep the covenant.7 It is for this reason the sages instituted that there be a seat of honor for Elijah at every circumcision.8 At the brit milah we pray that just as Elijah came to testify in our favor, so may he come and herald the final redemption! Malachi 3:1. I Kings 19:2. I Kings 19:9–10. See Shir Hashirim Rabbah 1:6. See Shir Hashirim Rabbah ibid. Ibid.; Zohar 1:93a. See Ramak, Ohr Hachamah on Zohar ibid. See Pirkei d’Rabbi Eliezer 29, and Zohar ibid.
