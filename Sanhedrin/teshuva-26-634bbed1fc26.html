Answer

the four levels of creation are traditionally categorized as domem (inanimate), tzomeach (vegetative), chai (animal), and medaber (human with speech) The term 'memallel' is sometimes used as a designation for the human level with speech capacity, emphasizing the faculty or potential to emit words or utterances. [1] [2]

humans can convey complex abstract ideas through language [2]

The suggestion that 'memallel' refers to one who utters words, regardless of whether those words are consciously heard or accepted, while 'medaber' implies speech that is actually heard or accepted, is a hypothesis without clear classical source support There is no explicit authoritative text differentiating these according to experiential reception or acceptance of speech. [1] [2]

Still open

  • Explicit traditional Hebrew linguistic sources distinguishing precise definitions of Memallel, Medaber, and M'Siach with respect to spoken and accepted speech,
  • Authoritative classical sources explaining specifically why 'Memallel' is chosen as the label for the fourth kingdom/level of creation beyond general speech faculty
  • No explicit classical Hebrew or kabbalistic sources distinctly define Memallel vs Medaber vs M'Siach by whether speech is heard or accepted.
  • No direct source explains exactly why 'Memallel' is chosen as the label for the fourth kingdom beyond the general fact humans are speaking beings.

[1] Why Are Tefillin From Animal Hide?

chabad · Yehuda Shurpin

Many of the laws and details regarding the creation of tefillin are oral traditions that trace back to Moses, known as halachah leMoshe miSinai.1 Received from G‑d atop Mount Sinai, they were passed down by Moses to Joshua, and from there down to the leaders and sages of each generation. These specifications are not hinted at in Scripture, nor can they be deduced logically. Yet they are far from arbitrary. As Maimonides writes, it is appropriate to contemplate these decrees and provide an explanation for them whenever possible. With this in mind, here are just a few explanations that the mystics give as to why tefillin needs to be made of animal hide. Harnessing Impulses When we put on tefillin, we place one box upon the left arm (signifying action) opposite the heart (the seat of the emotions) and the other upon the head (the seat of the intellect). When doing so, we have in mind the submission of one's mind, heart and actions to the Almighty. On the one hand, we do not simply follow the impulses of the heart (since we can easily be tempted), but on the other hand, we can’t simply follow our intellect (as that path can be harsh and cold). Based on this, some explain that by placing sacred leather parchments in a box made of animal hide, we subdue and transform our animalistic desires and impulses and use them in our service of G‑d. Elevating All of Creation The mystics explain that the world can be separated into four major components: domem (inanimate/mineral), tzomeach (vegetation), chai (animal) and medaber (humankind). In the process of preparing the hides and making the tefillin, certain herbs (tzomeach) and water (domem) are used to process the hides (chai). When the person (medaber) performs the mitzvah of tefillin, he is gathering and elevating all four components of creation and demonstrating how G‑d is the master and creator of all.2 See Menachot 35a. See talk by the Lubavitcher Rebbe, 19th of Kislev 5743, Torat Menachem 5743, vol. 2, p. 661; see also Rama, Torat Ha'olah.

Answer 2 (Rabbi Kaii): Sorry, I don't know how to answer this without "essay" format. It's a very good question, but to properly answer it, there is a lot to get through. I have done my best to not condense it too much, and I welcome questions, and suggest reading up the citations for further study. The Name of Adnut, Lordship, should be taken to mean that Hashem is the absolute Master of nature. He created it and is creating it every moment, from absolute and utter nothingness, and is therefore in complete control. This is true Lordship (see many places, e.g. Sefer Maamarim 5659 Sukkot Night 2 ). This is why it is sometimes also associated with the aspect of " din " (it contains those letters in it - see R' Yosef Chayyim's Aderet Eliyahu on Parashat Shoftim ; See also the Baalei Berit Avram on Parashat Shoftim ), because "natural law" appears to be "logical". "Survival of the fittest", "one must earn one's bread with the sweat of one's brow" etc. Justice is cold, harsh, but fair - but certainly not "compassionate". If you combine the Name with "E-l", you get the same gematria as "tzav", which the Arizal associates with idolatry. In this world, we can look around, and say "where's the compassion?" and make a mistake and think that there's no Hashem, and this is the essence of idolatry (see Derech Mitzvotecha Mitzvat Korban Pesach ). However, there is a famous combination of the Names Adnut and Havaya, which is taken to mean that we restore this seeming schism when we look closely and see that everything actually is Hashem's compassion - Havaya is all there is. Shem Adnut also has the same gematria as "Heichal", i.e. the Beit Hamikdash, the seat of Hashem's righteous judges, who issue righteous judgements, and this creates this very combination of the Names, in a "higher unification" ( Chomat Anach on Shoftim 2:18 - see what I wrote here for more). Interestingly, the Beit Hamikdash is the one place where we pronounce Hashem's Name, instead of pronouncing it b'Shem Adnut! By studying Hashem's Laws (i.e. His righteousness and justice), we see the compassion. It's only right that if I have more money than I need, and someone else has less than they need, that I should give him some of my money. It's fair (tzeddaka = tzedek), but it's also a clear demonstration of how when we follow Hashem's laws, we are producing compassion*. Another example are the laws of slavery, i.e. treat your slave like a mentsch. It's only fair, but it shows the deep compassion we should have for every human being. See Hemshech Samech Vav , especially the later ma'amarim for extensive coverage on this concept of how halacha reveals Hashem. See also Likkutei Torah of the Alter Rebbe on Parashat Bechukotai . If you want to get specific, and go deeper (as your question and comments indicate), this Name is the name of Malchut of Atzilut. Much of what we said above is very similar to other vertlach about Elokim. I.e. when we see through the "mask" of the world, that all is Him, we are "shown to know, that Hashem is Elokim... Ein Old Milvado" (Devarim 4:39). So what's the similarity with Elokim and Adnut? Well, they both pertain to the obscuring, the "tzimtzum" Hashem creates in order for there to be this world. Elokim is His Name of Gevura, which represents His power of restriction and limitation. He uses it in order to restrict His Light so that the world, paradoxically, can be revealed . A nice mashal used is that of the sun. If the earth were inside the sun, we would not be able to see it, as it would be totally outshined by the sun's light. Therefore, the earth is at a distance from the sun, and only lit up by "rays" that have left the sun and travelled a long way, and now we can see the earth (see Derech Mitzvotecha Haamanut Elokut 6). This is another way of saying that if we saw just how good and compassionate Hashem is, we would lose our free will in that we would choose Him without a second thought, but because He "hides behind cold, harsh nature", we have an opportunity to choose Him from our own free will. So, Elokim is like a "shield" to Havaya (see Tanya Shaar Hayichud VehaEmuna ), restricting our perception so we are not blinded and nullified by Hashem. This is where Shem Adnut comes in. Hashem's Malchut is His power of Speech - He speaks the world into creation. In order for there to be a world that is, so to speak, "separate" from Him, He needs to use His power of Gevura - as above - and condense His Light and Being into letters, speaking words, as it is written in Sefer Maamarim 5659 Sukkot Night 2: וז״ש כבוד מלכותך כו׳ וגבורתך ידברו, דבכדי שיהיו האותיות דמלכות בבחי׳ יש, שיוכל להיות מקור להוות עולמות בי״ע, זהו ע״י וגבורתך דוקא, בחי׳ גבורה דז״א, לצמצם את האור, ועי״ז דוקא נעשו האותיות בבחי׳ יש כו׳ And this is the meaning of what is written: "The glory of Your kingship... and Your might they will speak." For in order that the letters of Malchut should be in the category of "somethingness", such that they can be the source for the coming into being of the worlds of Creation (BiYA), this is specifically through "Your Might," the aspect of Gevura of Ze'ir Anpin, to contract the light. And specifically through this the letters become in the category of "somethingness", etc. Why? Well, words are very superficial and external in comparison to the concepts they are representing - like a mashal. They are "just" words, and therefore this is a great contraction and restriction of Hashem's Light. It is through these words that He creates the world we live in, i.e. the world of nature that I spoke about above. The fact that He is speaking the world into creation is indeed why He is the absolute and utter Master and Lord of the world (it's His story!), hence the Name Adnut. For a bit more detail - the power of Malchut is the most external power of Hashem's Being. The world of Atzilut is not a "mashal", it is genuinely His very Being, although it has being somewhat restricted and limited by Gevura. What this means is that He identifies with Atzilut, He speaks in the first person. "My Chochma", "My Gevura", "My Malchut". Still, the worlds created by His Malchut are from Him, but He considers them "external" to Him - we are separate from Him. Therefore there is a transition point and this happens in Malchut of Atzilut, part of which actually becomes the highest point of the created worlds. This is Shem Adnut, and is also referred to as the Shechina, the Kise HaKavod, Mimaaleh Kol Almin, and the parsa / parochet . It is where Hashem "puts on His mask" as it were ( hester panim ; see Worldmask by R' Tatz ), and hides behind nature. We must know though that nature is a perfect mashal of Him (i.e. the words He speaks to create the mashal are all about Him, the Nimshal, authentically), and therefore if we study it through the lens of Torah, and particularly, study His Laws (as we we wrote above), we will see through the mashal, and create gilui panim ; this Yichud Elyon cited by Chomat Anach above. tl;dr: Havaya - Hashem's Name that is utterly beyond. Pertains to "being", but also pertains to His lofty goodness and compassion. Elokim - Hashem's power of Gevura - restriction and limitation. Adnut - Hashem's Name that is associated with His power of action and creation (Malchut), where, after He limits Himself by hiding behind nature, His Kingship is revealed as His being utterly in charge of nature - Lording over it. Our job is to realise that Hashem's goodness is still here in the world of nature, and is only hidden in order to allow free will. We study His Torah, and realise that the Master of Nature is actually one and the same as Havaya, the Infinite, Transcendent Havaya, Who is Good and Compassionate. Nature only exists due to His immense power of Gevura that gives us this impression, but in truth, He is Elokim, and the Master of Nature - all is One. Answer 3 (Mr E.): B"H In Tanya, Shaar Hayichud vihaemunah chapter 7 , the Alter Rebbe contrasts the names Adnus and Havayah, saying that the former is how Hashem is manifested within the division of space and time (malchis, kingship), while Havayah refers to how He creates all past, present and future as one essential point: Chapter 7 פרק ז The Alter Rebbe explained in the previous chapters that the Scriptural statement that “in the heavens above and upon the earth below, there is none other” is not intended to negate the existence of another god. Rather, it tells us that Divine Unity is such that there is no true existence other than G‑d, for all created beings are completely nullified in relation to Him and are united with Him. This is not perceived by them nor manifest in them only because of the tzimtzum which conceals the Divine life-force that continuously brings about their existence ex nihilo. Being unable to perceive this life-force, they consider themselves to be independently existing entities. This concealment, of course, applies only to created beings but not to their Creator. From the Divine perspective, there is no concealment whatsoever, inasmuch as Havayah and Elokim are truly one: the concealment effected by Elokim thus does not act as a concealment for Havayah. The Alter Rebbe will now explain how the above enables us to understand the teaching of the Zohar that Shema Yisrael is “higher-level Unity” and Baruch shem is “lower-level Unity.” Were Divine Unity merely to signify the existence of one G‑d, it would be impossible to speak of higher and lower levels of Unity. According to the above explanation, however, that Divine Unity means the nullity of created beings and their unity with G‑d, it is indeed possible to speak of two diverse levels of Unity. In this sense, “higher-level Unity” refers to the Divine faculty of creative speech (otherwise known as malchut), considered at the stage in which it is still found within its source. By way of analogy, the seminal letters of a person’s speech are first encapsulated within his thought and emotions, which will ultimately be responsible for his subsequent speech. (The Rebbe notes that “this was explained in Part I, chs. 20-21.”) The same is true Above: When supernal creative speech is at the stage in which it is still included within the supernal attributes—which, being infinite, are too lofty to serve as a source of creation, for it is inherently limited to space and time—creation as it exists in its source is united with G‑d at the higher level of Unity. Its manner of nullification is then similar to the nullification of the sun’s rays as they are found within the sun-globe. When, however, the Divine creative power of speech (or malchut), after undergoing a process of tzimtzumim, descends to a level at which it is able to serve as a source for creation, then the term “lower-level Unity” applies. Created beings at this level cannot be said to be completely and utterly nullified. For inasmuch as this level becomes the actual source of created beings, they must perforce have some measure of identity in relation to it. Although this level, too, is pervaded by the Ein Sof-light which unites with the Divine faculty of creative speech, this unification is nevertheless one of “lower-level Unity,” wherein created beings are seen to have some measure of existence—albeit a nullified form of existence, but existence nonetheless. When the soul leaves the body, the body immediately inanimates and decomposes. Likewise, the body sustains the animal soul in animals, and when the animal soul leaves the animal's body, it (the animal soul) ceases and nullifies. Likewise, for the nefesh tzomachat (plant soul) in plants. For the nefesh medaberet (human soul) we can say the same regarding its existing in this physical world, because immediately after the human soul leaves the body, it returns to its place in the higher worlds [as written (Eccl. 12:7) "Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto G-d who gave it."]. hence the animal soul is lower level than human soul and cannot exist independently of the animal body unlike human higher soul which is independent of the human body and can continue to exist without it. [6] What does midbar mean?

miyodeya

Answer 1 (Yaacov Deane): The noun Midbar is best translated as wilderness , not necessarily desert . The distinction being that a wilderness is a place devoid of human habitation and settlements. The term desert , although coincidentally usually not conducive to human settlement, is only about a lack of precipitation, rainfall or snowfall. And to emphasize, it doesn’t necessarily imply a lack of availability of water. A desert can be filled with wellsprings, underground aquifers or like the continent of Antarctica can be covered with ice and snow because whatever precipitation has ever fallen there over the millennia has not evaporated because of the extreme and continuous cold. So returning to the subject of wilderness being devoid of human settlement, the Torah, in its categorization of different types of creations, has four general categories of: inanimate objects דומם (things which are silent and do not have their own power of locomotion), plants צומח (things which sprout, usually, but not limited to sprouting from the ground), animals חיות (meaning things which do not sprout and which possess the power of self-locomotion. They may also be able to make noises and even possess some form of communication other than speech.) and humanoids מדבר (beings which have the power of speech, either oral or written or both). And this underlines the association of wilderness מדבר (pronounced Midbar) and humanoid מדבר (pronounced M’Daber, meaning speak or speech) settlement. That a wilderness can be profusely filled with all kinds of life, but is devoid of speech. Answer 2 (Shmuel): Rabbi Yonathan Sacks z"l explained once that the meaning behind the word "midbar" is to mean (COVENANT & CONVERSATION The Wilderness and the Word, Bamidbar 5768) : The Hebrew word midbar, wilderness, has the same root as the word dabar/davar, meaning “word” or “thing.” It has the same letters as medabber, “speaking.” It is in the wilderness that the Israelites hear revelation, the word or speaking of G-d There's a famous Midrash (see for example here ) that explains why the Torah has been given in the midbar. Why was the Torah not given in the Holy Land, Eretz Yisrael? The answer lies, tells the Midrash, in the fact that a midbar has no ownership on itself. A midbar belongs to no one. So too, everyone has an equal claim to the Torah (see also Midrash Tanchuma, Chukas 21:1) Elsewhere , Rabbi Sacks explains the deeper meaning behind the reason why the Torah was given in the midbar: The giving of the Torah in the wilderness is an essential feature of Jewish history. Had the Israelites received the Torah in the land, it would be indissolubly associated with the land. Exile would mean the end of the covenant. It would make no more sense to keep Torah while in exile than to obey the laws of Russia while living in Spain. What made the G-d of Israel different was the fact that He was sovereign of the universe, not a local deity. That is why the Jewish people survived dispersion. Only the G-d of everywhere can be found and worshipped anywhere. I would strongly recommend this article, to further grasp the essence of the word "midbar" and the relationship between the giving of the Torah and us being in exile. https://www.rabbisacks.org/covenant-conversation/bamidbar/wilderness-and-revelation/

[7] Rabbinical explanation of the physiological resemblance between animals and humans

miyodeya

Answer 1 (Joel K): R. Chaim Vital, Etz Chaim 42:1 : מ"ש חכמי הטבע כי בין הדומם והצומח הוא הקורא"לי הנקרא אלמוגים ובין הצומח והחי הוא אדני השדה הנזכר במסכת כלאים שהוא כמין כלב גדל בקרקע וטבורו נשרש בקרקע ויונק משם וכשחותכין הטבור שלו מת ובין החי והמדבר הוא הקוף .‏ [T]hat which the scientists wrote that intermediate between inanimate matter and plants there is coral, and between plants and animal life there is the adnei hasadeh (mentioned in Masechet Kilayim) which is a kind of dog that grows from the ground and which is umbilically connected to the ground from which it is sustained and if one cuts that cord it dies, and intermediate between animals and humans is the monkey . So, if apes are viewed as an intermediate category between animals and humans, it makes sense that they would resemble the human form in certain ways. Answer 2 (Joel K): If you’re looking for practical ramifications of the resemblance between humans and apes, then consider R. Yose’s opinion in Kilayim 8:5 : וְאַדְנֵי הַשָּׂדֶה, חַיָּה. רַבִּי יוֹסֵי אוֹמֵר, מְטַמְּאוֹת בָּאֹהֶל כָּאָדָם.‏ The adnei hasadeh is a wild animal. R. Yose says, it[s carcass] imparts impurity in a tent, like a human. What is this adnei hasadeh creature? Tiferet Yisrael ad loc. : בר נש דטור. נ"ל דר"ל וואלדמענש הנקרא אוראנגאוטאנג והוא מין קוף גדול בקומת וצורת אדם ממש. רק שזרעותיו ארוכים ומגיעין עד ברכיו ... מיהו ת"ק ס"ל דאע"ג דדומה לאדם בפרצופו ואבריו. אפ"ה כשמת דינו רק כשאר נבילות. ואמ"ט באהל.‏ This refers to the “mountain man”. It appears to me that it means the “wildman” which is called orangutan. This is a type of large ape, of the actual size and appearance of a human, except that it has long arms which reach to its knees ... However, the Tanna Kamma holds that although it is similar to a human in its face and limbs, nevertheless, when it dies it is subject to the same rules as other animal carcasses, and does not impart impurity in a tent. So, according to Tiferet Yisrael, R. Yose holds that carcasses of orangutans (and maybe other great apes as well?) have some of the same laws of impurity as human corpses (and which do not apply to other animals) owing to their physiological similarities. The other sages, in contrast, believe that orangutans' similar appearance to humans is not sufficient cause for their carcasses to impart impurity in the same way as humans corpses do.
