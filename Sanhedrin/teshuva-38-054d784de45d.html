Question

The two "sins" of Issachar

Jacob's son Issachar (יִשָּׂשכָר) is written with two "sins", yet only one is pronounced. Why is that?

Answer

The traditional explanation is that the name is spelled with two ש letters, but one is not sounded because the second shin is treated as a silent, non-pronounced letter in the Masoretic reading of the name. [4]

One classic explanation says the two shins represent two different meanings in Leah’s words: one points to “God has given me my reward,” and the other to the phrase about having hired Jacob, which is described there as a shameful expression; therefore that second shin is written but not read. One classic explanation says the two shins represent two different meanings in Leah’s words: one points to “God has given me my reward,” and the other to the phrase about having hired Jacob. [1] [3]

That source also notes that this follows the reading tradition of בני אשר, and records that some traditions vocalized it differently. [1]

The broader context is that the verse itself connects the name to Leah’s statement about “reward,”. And the main point of the commentaries is to explain the unusual spelling and pronunciation together. So the name is not being read as a straightforward phonetic spelling, but as a name with an extra written letter that carries meaning beyond the actual sound. [6] [2] [4]

The supported answer is: because the received reading tradition treats one shin as silent,. While the two written shins are explained as carrying extra meaning—either two separate word-meanings or a later name-history connected to Yov/Yeshuv. The sources do not give one single universally stated reason, but preserve these alternatives. [4] [1]
