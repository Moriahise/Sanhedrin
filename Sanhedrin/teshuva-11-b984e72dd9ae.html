Question

Minhag to shake Arba Minim every time they are taken

Years ago I saw that the Rav and others in a shul I was in would shake the Arba Minim when they picked them up for Hoshanos (when done after Mussaf) as well as Hallel (assuming they weren't saying the Bracha at that time, in which case they'd be shaking them anyway). At the time I was told there is a minhag to do this, and I subsequently saw it in writing somewhere, but since then I haven't been able to find this. Does anyone know of or have this minhag and know of a source? Similarly there is a minhag to shake them before switching to the Hoshanos bundle on Hoshana Rabba, and then shaking that bundle in the same way, which I assume has the same or similar source. Thank you and Gut Moed

OpenAI formulation from saved sources

The Gemara in Sukkah 43b-44b discusses the practice of striking (חיבוט) the aravah branches on Hoshana Rabbah, describing it as מנהג נביאים (a custom instituted by the prophets) or יסוד נביאים (a practice established by them). Rashi interprets חביט to mean waving, while Rambam understands it as beating, which should be done on the ground (Tur and Rema in Orach Chaim 664:4 likewise distinguish between waving and beating). The Mishnah Berurah (664:19) explains that this beating is done first on the ground (five times, following Arizal's Kabbalistic custom) and then on a vessel to clean the leaves. This practice is intended to symbolically diminish harsh judgement and express joy. This source supports a custom of shaking or beating the aravah branches outside of the blessing and mitzvah performance, indicating a layered tradition concerning the handling of the aravah at specific times (2). [2]

HaRav Chaim Vital, quoting the Arizal, writes in his Peri Etz Hayim (Sha'ar HaLulav, Chapter 3) that during Hallel, the arba minim are shaken in all six directions multiple times, totaling 72 shakes per day (three shakes in each of the six directions four times during Hallel), symbolizing the attribute Hesed. This indicates a kabbalistic and minhag-based practice to shake the minim at multiple moments in the Hallel service, even beyond the initial mitzvah with the blessing. This accords with the observed practice of shaking the arba minim when taken for Hallel even if no blessing is recited at that time (3). [3]

Nitei Gavriel (Hilchos Arbas HaMinim 44:3) notes that during Hallel and Hoshanos one fulfills the mitzvah when using a borrowed lulav (and by extension the regulations of 'lechem' — concern over benefiting from the four species — do not apply strictly). The minima taken for Hallel are only a rabbinic-level performance (as opposed to the first day of Sukkot, when the mitzvah is biblical), and thus these leniencies allow shaking the arba minim multiple times, potentially before and after Hoshanos without concern for halachic prohibitions regarding interruptions or blessings. This supports the permissibility of shaking the arba minim at each taking, including when switched to the Hoshanos bundle on Hoshana Rabbah (4). [4]

The Mishnah Berurah and Shulchan Aruch (Orach Chaim 392:3) forbid speaking between the Tekios (shofar blasts), but do not explicitly prohibit speaking or interrupting between the lulav portion and the Hoshanos section in the Sukkot prayers. The Igros Moshe (OC II:36) discusses the seriousness of interruptions during Tekios but does not equate that with the parts of Sukkot involving lulav and Hoshanos. This lessens concern over interrupting activities, such as shaking the arba minim again when switching to the Hoshanos bundle, especially when there is a known minhag to do so. Thus, the minhag to shake the arba minim before and after switching to the Hoshanos bundle on Hoshana Rabbah is consistent with halachic considerations of interruptions (5). [5]

Regarding the timing of taking up the arba minim for different parts of the service, Tosafot on Sukkah 39a and the Shulchan Aruch (Orach Chaim 651:5) discuss the preferred order to pick up the lulav and make the blessing to avoid blessings said without the mitzvah in hand. Common practice is to pick up the arba minim first, then make the bracha, and then perform the waving. This may explain why the arba minim are picked up when changing from Mussaf to Hoshanos, or from Hallel to Hoshanos, triggering the mitzvah performance and enabling shaking at those times. This custom clarifies why shaking may be observed upon taking the minim each time, even outside the initial mitzvah lesson (6). [6]

The source is the Gemara, Sukkah 43b-44b, which calls this practice מנהג נביאים (a custom instituted by the prophets) or יסוד נביאים (a practice established by them). (The Gemara ends up ruling that the first version is correct, and that therefore we don't say a berachah for it.) On 43b and 44b the Gemara mentions, in this connection, חיבוט of the aravah. (The following mishnah, on 45a, also mentions the opinion of R. Yochanan ben Berokah that in the Beis Hamikdash, they would חובטין palm branches - rather than aravos - on Hoshana Rabbah.) Rashi on 44b (ד"ה חביט) understands this to mean "waving," but Rambam (Hil. Lulav 7:22) says that it means "beating," and says that it can be done "on the ground or on a utensil"; Rema (Orach Chaim 664:4), citing Tur, says that we do both - first wave it and then beat it. Kabbalistically, it should be beaten specifically on the ground (Zohar 3:32a). Mishnah Berurah (664:19) compromises: first beat it on the ground (five times, in keeping with a statement by Arizal), then continue beating it on a utensil to knock off the leaves. Reasons behind this practice: Taamei Haminhagim cites Tolaas Yaakov that it is "to destroy the [severe] judgement so that it not be found, and bring it down to the dust so that it ceases to exist from the world." (In Kabbalistic writings they speak of the "five severities," which is why Arizal says to use five aravah branches and to beat them five times.) In a footnote there he also cites Levush, that it is done as an expression of joy.

[6] Why pick up the arba minim prior to making the bracha?

miyodeya · {'accept_rate': 83, 'account_id': 10902486, 'display_name': 'Joel K', 'link': 'https://judaism.stackexchange.com/users/14850/joel-k', 'profile_image': 'https://www.gravatar.com/avatar/ad6ff7de19bf310a4c2246f1e0902fea?s=256&d=identicon&r=PG&f=y&so-version=2', 'reputation': 50442, 'user_id': 14850, 'user_type': 'registered'}

Tosafot Sukkah 39a s.v. over la-assiyatan write: מטעם זה צריך לברך אלולב קודם שיטלנו דאי לאחר שנטלו מדאגבהיה נפק ביה כדאמרי' בסוף פירקין (סוכה דף מב.) ומיהו לא מסתבר כלל דהיאך יברך עליו והוא מונח בכלי הא אמר בהקומץ רבה (מנחות לה: ושם) תפילין מאימתי מברך עליהן משעת הנחה עד שעת קשירה וכל שעה שאין המצוה מזומנת בידו לעשות לא מיסתבר כלל לברך עליו To summarise their point: while it is true that at first glance one would expect that the correct procedure would be to make the blessing before picking up the lulav , it actually doesn’t make sense to make the blessing before the mitzvah item is in one’s hand and ready for the performance of the mitzvah . Tosafot go on to offer three suggestions (two of which you list in your question) as to how one can make the blessing while the lulav is in his hand but before fulfilling the mitzvah . Rambam Hilchot Lulav 7:6, on the other hand, does arguably write to do exactly as you suggest - first make the blessing and then pick up the lulav (although even this is not totally clear cut - compare with what he writes in Hilchot Berachot 11:15, where he allows and possibly prefers that the blessing be recited after picking up the lulav ). For the sake of completeness, I’ll note the opinion of Ran (Sukkah 20b midapei harif s.v. umiderparchinan ) who writes that one simply makes the blessing after picking up the lulav even though he has already fulfilled the mitzvah , because he still has yet to wave it and complete the mitzvah totally. Shulchan Aruch Orach Chaim 651:5 rules like the first two suggestions of Tosafot (blessing between picking up the lulav and the etrog , or blessing while holding the etrog upside-down) and Beiur HaGra (quoted in Mishnah Berurah :25) prefers the third suggestion of Tosafot (holding the lulav with intent not to fulfill the mitzvah until after the blessing is made). So, that’s why common practice is as you see, rather than making the blessing before picking up the lulav .
